Reports a class whose hand-written equals and hashCode read different state. Folding the superclass in counts as state, so an equals calling super.equals beside a hashCode that skips super.hashCode is the same finding as a dropped field.

The two directions are different problems and report at different severities.

  • Warning - hashCode reads what equals ignores. A contract break. Two objects the relation calls equal differ in that member, hash apart, and land in different buckets, so one of them cannot be found in any HashMap or HashSet that holds the other.
  • Weak warning - equals compares what hashCode ignores. Legal: equal objects still agree on the smaller set, so their hashes still agree. The cost is collisions rather than correctness, which is why it is reported quietly - but it is almost always a member that was added to one of the pair and never to the other.

Both are the one shape @EqualsAndHashCode cannot adopt. The annotation drives both members from a single selection and a single callSuper, so a split member set has no spelling in it. Where the goal is to replace a hand-written pair with the annotation, this check is the inventory of what has to be decided first.

No quick fix is offered, and that is the finding rather than a gap in it. Closing the split means deciding which of the two member sets is the value of the object, and nothing in the source says which. A fix that guessed would either widen what equality means or rewrite a hash the author had a reason for - and a deliberately loose hash is a legitimate, if unusual, thing to have written.

The check declines to report rather than guess. A pair that delegates to a helper method, or reads state through a call this analysis cannot follow, is left alone: an unreadable body yields no member set, and comparing an unknown against a known one would report every such class. A field assigned inside hashCode is treated as a memoized hash rather than a member, so a cached hashCode does not report. A class already carrying @EqualsAndHashCode is left to the inspection that owns that annotation.
